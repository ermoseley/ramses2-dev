Dual-frame moment method — where it stands, and what to do next

Four-rung study of the finite-time-blowup construction · branches dfmm_3d_metal (27 commits) and dfmm_3d_cuda (4) · all figures below are from runs in this repository, not sketches
This page is written to be readable without prior exposure to kinetic theory. §0 builds up the physics and defines every symbol; if you already know what a moment closure and a realizability cone are, skip to §1.

0. Background — what this is about

The physics question

A famous open problem asks whether a smooth fluid velocity field, evolved by the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations, must stay smooth forever — or whether it can develop infinite velocity gradients in finite time. That event is called a finite-time blowup or a singularity.

A recent construction (a 166-page manuscript, our Ref. [1]) exhibits a velocity field that blows up at a specific time, which we call t_star. As t approaches t_star, the flow concentrates into a shrinking core, and the rate at which fluid elements are being stretched there grows like 1/Δ, where

Δ = t_star − t  =  the remaining time.

So the whole story is about what happens as Δ gets small.

Why a gas dynamicist should be suspicious

Navier–Stokes is not a fundamental law. It is the first-order approximation to the Boltzmann equation, which describes the actual gas of molecules. That approximation is only valid when molecules collide often compared to the timescale on which the flow changes — i.e. when the gas is close to local thermodynamic equilibrium.

But near a blowup the flow timescale is Δ, and Δ → 0. Sooner or later the collisions cannot keep up. At that point Navier–Stokes is no longer a description of a gas at all — it is just a partial differential equation that happens to be doing something dramatic.

The question this study asks: does the approximation break down before the blowup? If it does, then the singularity is a property of the equation rather than of any gas that could obey it.

The measurement: pressure is a tensor, and it cannot be negative

In kinetic theory pressure is not a single number. Write a molecule's velocity as the local mean flow u plus a random part c. The pressure tensor is

Pij = rho ⟨ci cj⟩

— an average over the molecules in a fluid element. That object is a covariance matrix of a probability distribution, and covariance matrices have a hard mathematical property: every eigenvalue is greater than or equal to zero. The reason is simple — an eigenvalue of P is rho times the variance of the molecular velocity along the corresponding eigenvector, and a variance cannot be negative.

The set of allowed P is therefore a cone in matrix space, called the realizability cone. A state inside it corresponds to some possible distribution of molecules; a state outside it corresponds to none.

This gives a clean, binary test, and it is the centre of the whole page. If a model predicts an eigenvalue of P below zero, it has predicted a negative variance. The model has falsified itself — no experiment, no tolerance, no judgement call required. The blowup note calls this its check 7, and lam_min(P) (the smallest eigenvalue of P) is the number plotted everywhere below.

What Navier–Stokes predicts, and the one knob K

Navier–Stokes assumes the stress responds instantaneously to the strain: Pi = −2μS0, viscosity times rate of deformation. (Pi is the anisotropic, trace-free part of P; S0 is the trace-free strain rate.) Substituting the blowup construction's strain field into that formula gives a strikingly simple answer:

lam_min(PNS) / p = 1 − K,   where  K = 8μ/(pΔ).

K is the single dimensionless group that controls everything here. It compares the collision time of the gas to the remaining time Δ: small K is a collision-dominated, safely-Navier–Stokes gas, and large K is a gas being deformed faster than it can relax. Because Δ shrinks as the blowup approaches, increasing K is our proxy for approaching t_star.

And the prediction is stark: Navier–Stokes gives a negative eigenvalue — an impossible gas state — the moment K exceeds 1. That is the claim to be tested numerically, and it is what §1 tests.

The alternative: moment methods, and the 2×2 design

If assuming the stress responds instantly is the problem, the fix is to stop assuming it. A moment method carries Pi as its own field with its own evolution equation: it relaxes toward −2μS0 over the collision time τ, but lags behind when the flow changes faster than that. Counting fields:

So any difference between rung 2 and rung 3 is attributable to the closure alone, which is the entire point of building it this way.

Hyperbolicity — the concept that decided the outcome

This one is worth understanding before reading §3, because it is what the study actually found.

For a system of conservation laws, the wave speeds are the eigenvalues of a matrix built from the equations (the principal symbol). If every eigenvalue is real, disturbances travel at finite speed and the system is called hyperbolic — the initial-value problem is well posed, which is the minimum requirement for a simulation to mean anything.

If instead a pair of eigenvalues becomes complex, a disturbance of wavenumber k grows like exp(|Im λ| k t). Note the k: the growth rate is proportional to wavenumber, so the finer the feature, the faster it explodes. This gives a diagnostic fingerprint that we lean on heavily:

Under-resolution gets better when you refine the grid. Ill-posedness gets worse. A simulation that fails harder on a finer grid, with the same physical setup, is not under-resolved — it is resolving a genuine instability of the equations it is solving.

Levermore's theorem is the key prior result: the ten-moment closure is hyperbolic wherever P is positive definite. In other words, for ten moments, staying inside the realizability cone is enough. There is no corresponding theorem for twenty moments — and that gap is exactly where this study broke.

1. The result

What survives, in one line. The Navier–Stokes closure predicts a negative principal pressure and the code confirms it, converged and quantitatively: min lam(P)/p → 1−K to within 1% at level 6, present from step 1, over a fixed fraction of the volume. The moment closure delays that state by a wide margin in K but does not prevent it — and the reason it does not is that the twenty-moment system stops being hyperbolic before it gets there. An earlier version of this page claimed the moment closure never goes negative; a resolution ladder overturned that. §3 has both the ladder and the mechanism.

How to read the chart. Each curve is one of the four rungs. Moving right along the horizontal axis means increasing K, which means getting closer to the singular time. The default indicator is check 7: the smallest eigenvalue of the pressure tensor, so crossing below the zero line means the model has predicted a state no gas can be in. The grey dashed line is the Navier–Stokes prediction 1−K. Hover any point for its exact value and cell count.

Setup: four rungs × six values of K, on a 163 grid (level 4), each run to t_star with output times clipped exactly onto quarters of it. Every plotted number is a minimum or maximum over the whole run, not a final-snapshot value.

What the check-7 panel says

Rung 2 tracks the Newtonian prediction and crosses zero between K = 1 and K = 2, exactly where the note says the extrapolation must fail — n(lam<0) reaches 1504 of 4096 cells at K = 3.

Rung 3 does not go negative at this resolution. Same grid, same initial condition, same Riemann solver, one namelist entry different (dfmm_closure='ns' → 'evolve'). n(lam<0) = 0 at every K, and min lam(P)/p saturates at 0.401 by K = 3 instead of falling to −1.8.

The reason is a term the Newtonian description simply does not have. Because the moment closure evolves Pi rather than reading it off the strain, its equation contains a term quadratic in Pi (written −[Pi·G]dev, where G is the velocity gradient). That term acts as a brake: the larger the anisotropy already is, the harder it pushes back. Newtonian stress has no such feedback, so it grows without limit as K rises — which is exactly what the max |Pi|/p panel shows.

This panel is level 4, and the rung-3 zero does not survive refinement. An earlier version of this page called rung 3’s zero “the falsifiable claim of the whole exercise, and it holds.” A resolution ladder since then shows otherwise: rung 3 is realizable at level 4 for K = 2, 3 and at level 5 for K = 2, but diverges at level 5 for K = 3 and at level 6 for both. Refining makes it worse, which is the signature of an ill-posed system rather than of under-resolution. Level 4’s zero was numerical diffusion holding the state inside a region the closure cannot sustain. See §3 for the mechanism and the restated claim.

The failure ordering at level 4 is: the phase-space rank indicator g collapses first (rung 3 at K = 3), Newtonian lam_min crosses second, evolved lam_min not at all. Only the first two survive refinement — and the g collapse turns out to be the early warning of rung 3’s own failure, not a separate phenomenon: at level 5, K = 3, min g hits zero at step 41 while min lam is still 0.367, and lam crosses only at step 157.

Read rung 1 as “not applicable”, not as a pass. It prints 1.000 at every K simply because it carries no Pi at all, so the quantity being plotted is identically 1 by construction rather than by any physics.

There is a deeper reason, and it is worth a moment. In incompressible Navier–Stokes the pressure is not a thermodynamic quantity: it is a Lagrange multiplier whose only job is to enforce div u = 0, and adding a constant to it everywhere changes nothing. So there is no absolute scale against which “the smallest principal pressure has gone negative” even means anything. Check 7 cannot be asked of incompressible Navier–Stokes, let alone answered. Rung 4 — incompressible flow with an evolved pressure tensor — exists precisely to make the question well posed.

2. Honest status of each rung

Which of the four cells of the 2×2 can currently be trusted, and over what range of K. “Gates” below means the suite of verification tests each rung must pass — analytic decay rates, conservation, known wave speeds — before any physics result from it is believed.

usableRung 1 — incompressible + Newtonian

Solved with a spectral (Fourier) method in double precision, with the incompressibility constraint imposed exactly rather than approximately. Checked against the Taylor–Green vortex, which has an analytic decay rate, to 6×10-7; the velocity field's divergence stays at 10-15, i.e. round-off.

Check 7 cannot be asked of this rung at all — see the note at the end of §1.

usableRung 2 — compressible + Newtonian

This is the Navier–Stokes comparison case, and it is deliberately built from the same code as rung 3 with the stress replaced by its instantaneous formula, rather than from a separately written viscous solver. That matters: it means the rung-2 versus rung-3 difference cannot be blamed on two different implementations. It reproduces the predicted 1−K.

usable at L4 onlyRung 3 — compressible + moments

Twenty moments plus the “dual frame” bookkeeping that tracks how fluid elements are being deformed — 38 stored fields per cell in all. All nine verification gates pass, and it is the rung the headline rested on — but it diverges under refinement: fine at level 4, and at level 5 for K = 2; diverges at level 5 for K = 3 and at level 6 for both. Same obstruction as rung 4 (§3). Sound at ten-moment order (DFMM=1).

blocked for K ≥ 1Rung 4 — incompressible + moments

Verified and trustworthy for K ≤ 0.5. Above that it diverges, and crucially the growth rate rises with refinement (38.4 per unit time at level 4 → 55.6 at level 5) — the ill-posedness fingerprint from §0, so a finer grid will not rescue it. P1 explains what it is.

This rung matters more than its status suggests: it is the only one that can ask check 7 of an incompressible flow, which is the case the blowup construction is actually about.

A scope limit worth knowing: we release a snapshot, we do not follow the construction

The construction is forced: a smooth body force confined in space and time keeps the flow on a trajectory whose strain at the origin is exactly S(0,t) = (1/Δ) diag(−2,−2,4) with Δ = t_star − t.

Our initial condition freezes that field at Δ = blowup_delta and then lets the gas evolve freely. So the K ladder is a proxy for approaching t_star: each rung of it asks “given the constructed state at remaining time Δ, does a gas immediately violate the kinetic assumptions?” The note asks the stronger question — along the constructed trajectory, do the assumptions survive to t_star?

Why this matters, in plain terms. The construction keeps its flow on a blowup trajectory by pushing on it — a body force, tuned so the stretching rate at the origin follows 1/Δ all the way to t_star. We do not have that force. What we do is take a photograph of the constructed flow at one instant, hand it to the gas, and let go.

So our runs answer “given the state the construction reaches at remaining time Δ, does a gas immediately violate the kinetic assumptions?” — whereas the sharper question is “travelling along the trajectory, do the assumptions survive all the way to t_star?” Increasing K is our stand-in for the second question, and it is a good one, but it is a stand-in.

This is the most important conceptual gap and it is addressable (P2). We cannot import the full 166-page velocity field, but we can prescribe the strain's own time dependence: the initial condition already sets the strain coefficient to 2/Δ, so making it 2/(Δ0 − t) and relaxing the velocity toward that field turns the study into a direct measurement of at what remaining time each check fires — which is exactly the deliverable the note frames.

The mechanism — why more moments made things worse, not better
The idea in words. One would naturally expect that carrying more kinetic detail (twenty moments instead of ten) can only help describe a gas far from equilibrium. What we found is the opposite, and the reason is well-posedness rather than accuracy.

For ten moments, Levermore proved that staying inside the realizability cone — keeping the pressure tensor a legitimate covariance matrix — is enough to guarantee real wave speeds. For twenty moments no such theorem exists, and it turns out not to be true: there are states with a perfectly respectable, positive-definite P whose wave speeds are nevertheless complex. The extra field Q that was supposed to buy extra fidelity is what carries the system out of the well-posed region. So the region where the twenty-moment model is usable is strictly smaller than the region where it is physical, and this problem's states fall in the gap.

Quantitatively: Levermore’s theorem gives hyperbolicity of the ten-moment Gaussian closure wherever P > 0. It says nothing about twenty. To test it we compute the wave speeds (the eigenvalues of the principal symbol, §0) on the states the code actually visited — reading each cell back out of the saved snapshots and diagonalising in double precision. On the snapshot where rung 3 first fails, taking the 200 most-strained cells out of 32768:

hyperbolicnon-hyperbolic
lam_min(P) ≥ 015232
lam_min(P) < 0016

Every unrealizable cell is non-hyperbolic, as it must be. But 32 cells are realizable and still non-hyperbolic, at lam_min(P)/p = 0.180 and |Pi|/p = 0.412 — comfortably inside the cone. So the hyperbolic region is a strict subset of the realizability cone.

It is driven by Q, not Pi. Scaling that cell’s Q → αQ gives max|Im|/|lam| = 1.2e−16 / 0.364 / 0.498 / 0.625 / 0.689 at α = 0 / 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.5 / 1.0. At α = 0 the state is a valid ten-moment state and is hyperbolic to round-off, exactly as Levermore requires. The mirror test — Pi → βPi at full Q — stays non-hyperbolic all the way to β = 0.

Mapping the boundary with that cell’s shapes, Q normalised by its thermal scale p3/2/rho1/2:

The hyperbolic region is a bounded neighbourhood of equilibrium in Q that shrinks to nothing exactly as lam_min(P) → 0, touching the realizability boundary only at Q = 0 where the ten-moment guarantee takes over. So rungs 3 and 4 fail for the same reason in different places: the twenty-moment closure has no hyperbolic neighbourhood large enough for the states this problem visits. Both are sound at DFMM=1 (ten moments), which is hyperbolic wherever P > 0 — so the 2×2 should be closed at ten-moment order, and the twenty-moment runs reported as what they are.

Two candidate explanations were tested and retracted. The apparent law |Q|max = 0.91 (lam_min/p)3/2 fits that one shape pair to 2% but six random shape pairs give slopes 0.16–1.17, nowhere near 1.5 — only the qualitative statements are shape-robust. And the equilibrium symbol’s repeated eigenvalues (±1.732 ×2, ±1.000 ×3, 0 ×6) are all semisimple (i.e. not defective — each has as many independent eigenvectors as its multiplicity), so there is a genuine hyperbolic neighbourhood of equilibrium and the “small Q splits a defective pair” hypothesis is wrong.

4. Agenda

Ordered by what could falsify or unblock the result, not by build effort. Items are labelled P1…P6 in that priority order and are referred to by those labels elsewhere on the page. Two are now done; their entries record what came back.

  1. Settle the rung-4 divergence with one offline eigenvalue test. done — it is the closure
    Done, and the answer is the first branch: the twenty-moment closure has lost hyperbolicity on the states this flow visits. The incompressible member loses it above |Pi|/p0 = 0.277–0.408 depending on strain shape; the compressible member (rung 3) loses it in a Q-dependent region inside the realizability cone. See §3. So this is a statement about the model, not the integration — and the corollary is that rung 3 does not survive either, which is what overturned the headline.

    Getting there required retracting a wrong first attempt: the original symbol finite-differenced the flux Jacobian alone, which gave the ten-moment fastest speed as the sound speed 1.291 instead of √3 and declared the Gaussian closure non-hyperbolic — contradicting Levermore. The error: −2p S0ij contains derivatives of u and belongs in the principal part. Rebuilt quasilinearly, the symbol reproduces √3 = 1.732051 and √(3+√6) = 2.334414 exactly. Two claims derived from the broken version were withdrawn.

    The runtime guard has been moved accordingly: it was |Pi|/p0 > 100, which fires only once the run is numerically dead and then reports −inf. It now warns at the hyperbolicity threshold and stops at lam_min(P) < 0. On rung 4 at K = 1 that converts “grows to 105, NaN” into a measurable crossing: warning at t = 0.0023, cone crossing at t = 0.2242 = t_star/2 with lam_min/p0 = −0.046 at |Pi|/p0 = 0.52, and E_trunc/E = 1.1e−13 there — a fully resolved crossing.
    Cost: spent. Result: rung 4 unblocked as a measurement; rung 3 reclassified.
  2. Follow the construction's trajectory instead of releasing a snapshot. new physics
    Prescribe Δ(t) = Δ0 − t and drive the velocity toward the constructed strain field, so the run approaches t_star along the trajectory rather than departing from it. Then the deliverable becomes what the note actually frames: the time, in units of Δ/t_c, at which each check fires, and their ordering. Recommend a blowup_forced namelist flag so the unforced runs stay reproducible, and an honest statement that this forces the local strain, not the full construction.
    Cost: days. Turns a proxy into the measurement. Highest scientific value.
  3. Resolution ladder on the headline. done — it flipped the answer
    Rungs 2 and 3 at K = 2 and K = 3, levels 4–6. This was the right test and it came back against the claim: rung 3 diverges at level 5 for K = 3 and at level 6 for both, while rung 2 converges onto 1−K. Numbers in §3. The reassurance that min lam(P)/p converges cleanly at K = 1 was, as this item itself warned, not the test.
    Cost: spent. Outcome: headline restated, rung 3 reclassified.
  4. Rerun the crossings at NPRE=8 on CUDA. precision
    Everything above is single precision twice over: the Metal build is NPRE=4 (Apple GPUs have no float64) and output_hydro.f90 writes real(…,kind=4) regardless of NPRE. An independent float64 recheck of the snapshots reproduces the kernel’s n(lam<0) exactly at every time-matched output — 48/48, 5696/5696, 0/0, 16/16 — so the diagnostic is verified, but the precision is not. Rung 2’s crossing at K = 3 is far too large to be precision-sensitive; rung 3’s level-5 onset (16 cells at lam/p = −0.29) is not, and should be confirmed at NPRE=8 before publication. This is what the CUDA branch is for.
    Cost: one A100 job. Blocks: the rung-3 onset number specifically.
  5. Verify AMR on the mass-like tower with an actually refined run. correctness risk
    When a grid refines, values must be interpolated down to the finer cells and averaged back up to the coarser ones. For the dfmm fields that has been verified by reading the code only, never by running a refined case. The tower (rho D_i, rho Sxx, rho Sxv) is mass-like where Pi and Q are density-like, and the blowup problem has a shrinking core — AMR is the natural way to resolve it and is untested. If refinement mishandles the tower, the deformation tensor and therefore Kn_local are wrong on refined levels, silently.
    Cost: one refined blowup run plus a check that |rho/det J − 1| does not jump at level boundaries.
  6. Finish the CUDA transport half. enables scale
    The source half and the timestep kernel are ported and compile on an A100 (DFMM=1/2/4, NPRE=8). What remains is the transport half: the slope-limited predictor step over the extended state, the approximate Riemann solver (HLL) for the twenty-moment flux, the upwind kernel for the passively advected fields, and the bookkeeping that keeps fluxes consistent across refinement boundaries. Until then gpu_dfmm_godunov refuses rather than falling through to gpu_godunov, which would leave the dfmm fields unadvected while the run called itself a dfmm run.

    One thing not to assume: the two-kernel split on Metal exists because 38 fields need 43840 B against Apple's 32768 B threadgroup limit. An A100 offers 164 KB of shared memory per block, so measure before reproducing it. Also: NPRE=8 makes the realizability diagnostics trustworthy without the float64 recheck that Gate 9 needed.
    Cost: weeks. Needed for level 7+, i.e. for P3 beyond level 6.
